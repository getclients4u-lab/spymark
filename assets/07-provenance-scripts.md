# The Provenance Scripts™

**Deliverable 7 · SpyMark Protection System™**

Word-for-word answers to the 12 hardest questions you'll be asked about spymarks, watermarks, and origin records. Memorize the shape; adapt the words.

---

**1. "What's a spymark? Is that a real word?"**

> It's a new word for a real thing. A watermark verifies authenticity or asserts ownership — visible, benign. A spymark is a hidden signal that makes your work traceable **without your knowledge or consent**. Google's SynthID can encode a 136-bit payload into a 512×512 image — enough for a database ID linked to a person. That's a spymark. The word exists because the difference matters.

**2. "Aren't you just helping people hide that they used AI?"**

> No — and this is the most important distinction in the whole system. Stripping removes **non-consensual tracking identifiers** from *your* output. Disclosing tool use is a separate act, and my system tells you to do it. I strip covert identity tracking; I never tell anyone to lie about their process. Those are opposite things.

**3. "Can you actually remove Claude's text watermark?"**

> Not reliably, and anyone who promises you a 100% remover is either lying or selling malware. That's already happened in the wild — watermark-removal apps turned out to be scams. What you *can* do is rewrite the text in your own voice until it's genuinely yours. Human editing is statistically noisy; noise is the honest defense. The seal — proof of your authorship — is what actually protects you.

**4. "So should I just stop using AI tools?"**

> That's a choice, not a rule. Use whatever tools make you faster. The requirement is that what you publish is *yours* — directed, edited, and defensible. The system works whether you use zero AI or a lot of it; it just requires you to document the human work honestly.

**5. "Won't my clients be suspicious if I mention origin records?"**

> The opposite. In a feed full of unmarked output, the creator volunteering proof is the credible one. You're not confessing — you're differentiating. "This comes with an origin record" reads as *professional*, not *guilty*.

**6. "Isn't this just C2PA / Content Credentials?"**

> C2PA is a signing standard — useful, but it certifies *a file*, and it's controlled by whoever signs. An origin record is *your* narrative: what you made, with what, and what human work went in. They're complementary. Use C2PA where it's available; always keep your own record.

**7. "What if a platform strips/alters my badge?"**

> Then your record still stands — the badge is the visible layer, the origin log is the durable layer. Publish your log on your own site or newsletter so the proof lives somewhere you control.

**8. "How do I prove a computer didn't write this?"**

> You can't prove a negative in general — nobody can, and be wary of anyone claiming otherwise. What you *can* prove is positive: a dated record of your process, your drafts, your edits, your decisions. Evidence of authorship beats claims about non-authorship.

**9. "Is this legal?"**

> Stripping metadata from your own creative work is normal practice — cameras, editors, and exporters do it by default. What's not okay is misrepresenting AI output as fully human when a client, employer, or regulation requires disclosure. Follow your jurisdiction and your contracts; the system's own rule is *disclose tools, strip tracking.*

**10. "My employer says I must disclose AI use. Do I still want this?"**

> Yes — even more so. If you must disclose, you also need to *prove human review*. An origin record is exactly that proof. Compliance and provenance are the same product here.

**11. "Why should I pay for this when exiftool is free?"**

> The tools are free; the system is what costs. You're paying for the model (Detect–Strip–Seal–Broadcast), the templates, the checklist, and the pricing language that turns provenance into revenue. Anyone can run exiftool. Almost nobody turns it into a premium.

**12. "What's my actual risk if I do nothing?"**

> Three: you publish identities you didn't mean to share; you get accused of AI slop with no defense; and you get priced down to machine level because buyers can't tell you apart. Doing nothing is a decision to accept all three.
